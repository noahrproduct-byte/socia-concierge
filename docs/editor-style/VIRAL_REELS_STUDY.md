# Viral Reels Edit Study

Nov 10, 2026 · @Umar İskandar

## How the 50 were picked

Fifty reels, ten business niches, ranked by likes inside each niche. I searched Instagram from your logged-in account for 14 phrases ("real estate reel", "luxury home tour", "gym edit", "fitness transformation reel", "restaurant reel", "cinematic food edit", "barber edit", "car detailing reel", "coffee shop reel", "med spa reel", "clothing brand edit", "roofing reel", "landscaping edit", "basketball edit", "small business cinematic edit"), read likes, comments, account and date off every result (about 560 posts), kept only reels, and took the top 7 real estate, 7 fitness, 7 food, 5 sports, 5 cars, 5 coffee, 4 clothing, 4 barber, 3 med spa and 3 home services.

Likes are the ranking metric because Instagram does not expose view counts on the web for other people's posts. Likes range from 6 M (a Michigan estate tour) down to about 3 K in the thinner niches (roofing, landscaping), so "viral" means viral for that niche.

Each reel was then downloaded and measured the same way as your own edits: ffprobe for length, frame size and frame rate; ffmpeg scene detection for cut times; shot-length statistics from the cut list; and 12 frames per reel read by eye for hook, text style, transitions and ending. Every editing claim below comes from those frames and numbers, not from the caption.

## Real estate (7)

The real estate winners are not fast. Five of seven run 1 to 2.8 s per shot, two are one continuous walk, and the text is a single serif line that stays up for the whole reel or sets up a reveal. The camera work (gimbal glide, drone, FPV) does the work the cuts do in other niches.

**1. wayup\_media, 6.0 M likes, 18.1 s, 8 shots.** Hook: static front elevation with a serif title "The Front VS The Back" for the first 3 s. Then drone orbit over pool and tennis court (3 s), a gimbal glide through the kitchen and bar (2.2 s each), a push into the home theatre, and a cut back to the drone for the reveal. Ending: a second title, "Can you guess the price?", over the widest drone shot for the last 3 s. Cuts land on a music beat every 1.5 to 2.2 s; no transitions, straight cuts only. Colour: cool blue-hour exterior, warm interior. The engagement bait is the question, not the editing.

**2. realestate\_with\_julia, 2.0 M, 11.5 s, one take.** A single handheld-gimbal walk from the entry, up past the staircase, into the living room and across to the kitchen island. No cuts at all. One small italic script caption, "dream home", sits centre-frame the whole time. Natural light, no grade. This is the "walk-in" format: the viewer is the buyer.

**3. hafezi.architects, 1.0 M, 14.1 s, 9 shots.** AI-rendered home (note the identical white roses in every shot). Hook: "AM I DREAMING" in spaced serif caps over the exterior, 2 s. Then a burst of six 0.1 s flash cuts at 1.9 to 2.6 s (the one strobe in this niche), followed by 1.1 to 1.4 s shots of each room. A second line, "I AM LIVING IN MY DREAM", at 4.7 s. Closes on the exterior at dusk with the pool. Every shot is a slow push-in on a still render.

**4. suasarealestate, 774 K, 8.5 s, 6 shots.** Hook: light rays through slatted ceiling, lower-third caption "when your house is all about natural light" that stays up the whole reel. A woman walks the stairs and sits on a stool; cuts every 1.1 to 1.3 s, each one a different angle on the same light. Ends on the widest shot of the double-height room. Listing details are in the caption, not on screen.

**5. stylarc, 711 K, 14.7 s, 13 shots.** Fastest of the group: 1 s per shot, 8 cuts per 10 s. Renders again, with a tiny STYLARC watermark bottom-left. Order: gate with supercar, aerial of the driveway, front door, chandelier foyer, dining, kitchen, living room, ten-car garage, pool, then the waterfront aerial held 2.5 s. No text. The one-second beat and the escalating scale (door, room, garage, whole estate) is the structure.

**6. chrisgranorealtor, 693 K, 11.7 s, 9 shots.** Skit format. Realtor hands keys to a couple, bold white caption with black outline quoting each line ("Enjoy your new home!", "Thanks! you too!", "Me too?!") for the first 7 s on one wide shot, then four 0.6 to 0.9 s cuts of the realtor sitting on their sofa, their bed, the bathroom and the kitchen. The joke is the edit: a slow dialogue beat followed by a fast montage.

**7. wayup\_media, 181 K, 7.7 s, 3 shots.** Blue-hour exterior (1 s), kitchen island glide (3.8 s), bathroom tub push (2.8 s). No text. The 2022 version of the format before the account added title cards and the price question.

## Fitness (7)

Fitness splits in two. The transformation reels (8, 9, 10, 12) are two or three long shots with a hard before/after cut and a plain caption carrying the numbers. The "edit" reels (11, 13, 14) are the opposite: typography, black bars, slow motion and a cinematic grade. The transformations got 3 to 8 times the likes of the edits.

**8. brinajean.fit, 2.0 M, 16.6 s, 2 shots.** A "stitch" format. Shot 1 (7.7 s): selfie video in a kitchen, dancing, with a bold three-line caption "TRANSFORMATION STITCH INCOMING" for the first 2 s. Shot 2 (8.9 s): same kitchen, same shirt, 8.5 months later, same dance, ends with a wave to camera. One hard cut on the beat at 7.7 s. No grade, no music edit, no other text. The match (same place, same clothes, same move) is the entire technique.

**9. shubham\_weightloss.world, 1.0 M, 10.1 s, 3 shots.** Split-caption format: "Brain Controlling You.." with pizza, chicken and beer emoji over the before photos, then "Vs", then "You Controlling Brain" with flexing emoji over the after. Stills, not video: five before photos held about 0.8 s each with two black flash frames between them (the two cuts at 5.6 s), then three after clips. Hard cut at 8.1 s to the final full-body shot. Plain white sans caption, centre-frame, with a black drop shadow.

**10. iampeachfit, 870 K, 10.8 s, 2 shots.** Shot 1 (3.8 s): bicep curl in 2021, lower-third caption "2021: Let's see where this takes me". Shot 2 (7 s): same curl, same angle, 2023, caption shortens to "2023:". The second shot is held for twice the length of the first; nothing else happens. Camera position and exercise match exactly.

**11. justcallme\_rush, 628 K, 12.5 s, 5 shots.** Landscape 16:9 letterboxed into 9:16 with black bars. Kinetic typography reel: one word per beat in a tall white condensed serif (BECOME, THE, PERSON, YOU, WANT), each word landing on a different 0.1 s gym clip (a 5-cut strobe at 2.2 to 2.7 s), then "DESTROY" in red at 10.4 s. Desaturated, crushed-black grade. The text is the edit; the footage is a background.

**12. iampeachfit, 325 K, 15.0 s, 3 shots.** Before (5.6 s, "My weight loss journey" and "315 lbs" top and bottom), cut at 5.6 s to after ("1.4 years later", "185 lbs"), second cut at 12 s to a flex. Same framing, same gym, same caption positions so only the body changes. 1.25 s longer than the 2021/2023 version and the numbers do the hook.

**13. dtcordonnier, 269 K, 15.4 s, 5 shots.** Tutorial reel. Title card "HOW TO FILM GYM VIDEOS" in a condensed serif over a 2.5 s establishing shot, then three labelled 3 s shots: "CLOSE UP: set the scene" (weight stack, cable), "ESTABLISHING SHOT: reveal the scene" (wide row), "CREATIVE SHOT: conclude the story" (low angle through the machine). 24 fps, warm film grade, small centred serif labels. The labels teach the structure the reel itself uses.

**14. huzzzairr.flx, 241 K, 30.2 s, 12 shots.** Letterboxed 2.39:1 cinematic. Dark gym, slow push-ins and rack-focus shots of 2.5 s each for the first 19 s with no text at all, then a 5-cut flash burst at 19.5 to 20.7 s and a kinetic text ending, one word per cut: "WORLD", "THIS", "HAS TO END". Heavy teal-black grade with grain. The 19 s of silence before the text is the patience the format asks for.

## Food and restaurant (7)

The one food reel that broke a million likes has zero cuts and no food in it: it is a 7 s staff skit with a POV caption. The cinematic food edits (20, 21) are the fastest-cut videos in the whole set and the least liked of the food group. The lesson for a restaurant client is that the joke or the moment beats the montage, and the montage is what agencies sell.

**15. shnoopimtl, 1.0 M, 7.4 s, one take.** Locked-off medium shot of two staff behind the counter. Bold white uppercase caption with black outline, three lines, top third: "POV: A CUSTOMER IS TELLING THEIR CHILD THAT WE HAVE NO MORE JUICE". Nothing moves for 4 s, then one of them produces a juice bottle from under the counter and both react. The hold is the edit; the payoff is at 60 percent of runtime.

**16. padimenguning\_id, 94 K, 11.9 s, 3 shots.** Staff carries a tray through a door (2.1 s), cut to an overhead of the tray held for 5.4 s while small serif dish labels ("Es Podeng", "Nasi Liwet Ayam Goreng", "Soto Mie Bogor") pop on one at a time with a pointer line, then back to the wide as she walks past camera, ending on the logo. Labels appearing one by one on a held shot replace cuts; the dish names are the content.

**17. duna\_restobar, 93 K, 9.7 s, 2 shots.** Skit again: a girl at a table raises the menu like a hand-up, mum reacts (3.6 s), cut to the empty table and a menu sliding into frame, then pancakes appearing on the plate by jump cut at 8.1 s. Flat, natural light, no text on screen (caption is in Russian). The "ask and it appears" gag is done with two hard cuts and a hidden jump cut.

**18. friendofafarmer, 76 K, 10.8 s, 7 shots.** Exterior of the restaurant held 4 s with a two-line serif caption "That place is so cute! I wonder what's inside" and a passer-by, then six 1 s shots: dining room, dish on the table, barista at the counter, framed print and jars, pancakes being drizzled, chalkboard sign. 24 fps, warm filmic grade. Cuts on a steady one-second beat after the 4 s hold.

**19. sasharowlee, 64 K, 24.8 s, 13 shots.** Behind-the-scenes POV: every shot is the camera operator's hands holding a cinema camera with the monitor showing the food shot being taken. About 1.8 s per cut, 13 setups: kitchen pass, plating, chef, a table of guests, a diner in red. No text. The format sells the service (food videography) by showing the rig, not the result.

**20. showmo.films, 56 K, 26.3 s, 37 shots.** The fastest reel in the set: 0.47 s median shot, 13.7 cuts per 10 s, nine flash clusters. Dark moody grade, steam and smoke, hands, knives, lanterns, cocktails, a wok flame, shrimp on a grill, then a hard cut to a flat grey end card with a tuk-tuk logo at 21 s held for 5 s. Cuts are beat-synced and alternate close detail with wide room. This is the agency showreel style; it got the fewest likes but the largest number of cuts.

**21. decadentdepictions, 40 K, 11.9 s, 12 shots.** Same school as 20, tighter: lantern (1 s), grill flame, searing beef with a ladle, dumplings from above, a "FORTU" menu card, fried chicken falling onto a plate, cocktail shaker, powder dusting, espresso martini, a bread roll pulled apart, steak, a diner sipping. 0.6 s median shot with three flash clusters in the first second and at 7.5 to 9.4 s. Every shot has motion inside it (pour, flame, pull), so the fast cut reads as rhythm rather than chaos.

## Sports edits (5)

These are the closest relatives of your NIBA montages, and they confirm the style: 0.4 s median shots, flash clusters, letterboxing, one slow-motion finish. The two slower ones (24, 26) replace cuts with a long slow-motion dunk and a single text card.

**22. x0tkin, 728 K, 17.1 s, 20 shots, square 1:1 letterboxed.** Opens with 6.8 s of a TV sketch (Sabrina Carpenter on SNL) with kinetic captions, one phrase per beat ("AND I THINK I MIGHT", "FOR BEING", "TURN MYSELF", "YOU'RE UNDER ARREST CAMERAMAN"), then the punch: a hard cut at 6.8 s into a LeBron Cavs montage at 0.3 to 0.5 s per shot with four flash clusters, mixing broadcast footage, a video-game render, a phone-recorded TV, and a 3D "23" graphic. The hook is a meme audio with its own captions; the edit only starts after the setup lands.

**23. gdfactoryclips, 306 K, 14.4 s, 21 shots, 30 fps.** Kobe tribute. Hook: 1.6 s of Kobe pointing, then a black card with "BLACK MAMBA" that re-types itself in three fonts (script, bold, plain) over 2.4 s, then 10 s of highlights at 0.43 s median with five flash clusters: scream, fadeaway, dunk from the baseline cam, jersey pop, point to camera, confetti. Slight zoom-punch on every cut (the frame scales 5 to 10 percent). Ends on the confetti shot. Tiny watermark "GDFACTORYCLIPS" centre-right.

**24. 29hlouis, 304 K, 22.9 s, 2 shots, 24 fps letterboxed.** Anthony Edwards. Shot 1 (10 s): a tunnel walk and warm-up in slow motion with a serif text stack top-right ("WHEN YOU IN YOUR PRIME", "IMAGINE", "BUCKLE UP") that changes every 2 s while the picture barely moves. Shot 2 (13 s): the dunk, slowed to about 25 percent, uncut, ending on the editor's name card "LOUIS" in serif caps. Two shots in 23 s is the opposite of a montage and it got the same likes as the 21-shot Kobe edit.

**25. azrovfx, 284 K, 46.6 s, 12 shots.** Long form for a reel. The first 31 s is one broadcast clip, letterboxed, with the commentary captions burned in (Curry hurt on the floor, walks off, comes back). The edit only begins at 31 s: eleven shots in 15 s, 1.4 s median, with a sharpen-and-glow grade, scream, celebration, point to the crowd. The story is 2/3 of the runtime and the "edit" is the reward.

**26. vandijk.0, 219 K, 15.2 s, 2 shots.** Two dunks: a 13.8 s slow-motion LeBron dunk (left-side view), heavy horizontal motion blur, grain, crushed blacks, then one hard cut to a second angle for the last 1.4 s. A "vandijk" watermark sits behind the player. No text. The whole reel is one slow-motion shot with a smear effect; the "edit" is grade and speed, not cuts.

## Cars and detailing (5)

Car detailing is the one business niche where the fast cinematic cut wins outright: the 2 M-like reel is 14 shots in 9 s with six flash clusters. The three cheaper phone-shot reels (29, 30, 31) all use the same trick, a before/after on every surface, and the two highest of those are also cut under 0.6 s.

**27. autolava, 2.0 M, 9.2 s, 14 shots.** Black BMW M4 in a white-tiled studio. Opens on a dead-centre front three-quarter with the shop logo on the wall, then a flash-cut (two frames) sequence: rear badge, front grille, reflections across the roof, tail light, fender, side profile, wide, mirror, wide again, grille close-up, and the last 1.2 s inside on the tan leather and steering wheel. Shots 2 to 13 are each 0.5 s with a 1 to 2 frame flash between; every shot is a slow slider or gimbal move so the car never sits still. No text; the logo is in the set.

**28. z1.concepts\_manjeri, 203 K, 16.3 s, 5 shots.** Green BMW M5 covered in snow foam against a black wall with the shop's name in neon. One static locked-off camera: the detailer wipes the rear (4.6 s), cut to a side angle of the wipe (4 s), cut to a wide of the bay (2 s), then back to the first angle as the clean car is revealed (3.5 s). A single tiny caption top-centre for the whole reel: "A place where men wait patiently..." The foam-to-shine reveal on a fixed camera is the structure.

**29. care4details, 80 K, 11.9 s, 19 shots.** Pure before/after, phone footage, 0.6 s per shot: filthy centre console then clean, stained white seat then clean, dashboard, door jamb, cup holders, pedals, window switches, screen. Nineteen hard cuts at 15 per 10 s, no transitions, no text, no grade. The dirt is the hook; the ping-pong between dirty and clean is the rhythm.

**30. dmv.detailers, 42 K, 13.1 s, 11 shots.** Black C63 at dusk. Exterior wide (0.8 s), foam on the hood (2.2 s), then 0.5 to 0.6 s cuts: rear, wheel, rear three-quarter, front wheel, car pulling out, car driving off, three static street shots as it leaves. Handheld, autumn light, no text. A dusk "the car leaves" ending instead of a reveal.

**31. elite\_kleanz\_detailing, 21 K, 14.8 s, 11 shots.** Satisfying-clean format, all extreme close-ups: drill brush in a cup holder (1.2 s), cut to the same spot rinsed, a detail brush on the gear surround, foam on the pedals, brush on the pedals, pedals clean. 1.2 s median, each pair of shots is a before and an after of the same inch. No text, no music cue visible; ASMR-style.

## Coffee shops (5)

Four of the five coffee winners are one shot or close to it, 5 to 10 s long, and every one of them has a small caption that carries the whole idea. The only montage (36) is also the only one that looks like an agency made it, and it ranked last in the group.

**32. cork.ge, 499 K, 7.7 s, one take.** Overhead POV of two people's hands, each holding a drink and a pastry (a pistachio matcha and an iced coffee). Static camera, 7 s. Lower-centre white sans caption that changes every 1 to 2 s as subtitles: "on today's episode", "we have", "two best friends", "two different styles", "but always the best vibes". ## What to feed Socia's editor from this

Your house style (the Socia Editor Style Study) is built from your own exports, which are mostly agency-grade montages: 2 s food beats, 0.75 s sports shots, hero holds. The viral set agrees on sports and car detailing and disagrees almost everywhere else. These are the rules that should be added or changed in `lib/studioClips/houseStyle.ts`.

1. Add a "one-take skit" genre. Six of the ten highest-liked business reels are a single locked shot with quoted-dialogue captions or a POV line, 7 to 12 s, payoff at 50 to 70 percent of runtime. The editor currently has no genre that allows zero cuts; `talking_head_reel` is the nearest and it is 40 to 45 s.
2. Add a "matched before/after" genre: two or three shots, same framing, label text ("Before", "2021:", "315 lbs") in the same position on both, hard cut, the after held longer than the before. This covers fitness, detailing, lawn, barber and salon work.
3. Cap default length at 12 s for food, coffee, med spa and home services. Your food\_reel band is 20 to 32 s; 26 of the 50 viral reels are 12 s or under and the only food reel above 20 s with high likes is a behind-the-scenes POV.
4. Make the first-second text line mandatory for business reels unless the genre is a visual-reveal one-take. The editor's `text` field already supports an opening line at 0 s; the rule should be that it is a POV, a quote, a question or a label, never a brand name.
5. Keep the sports rules as they are. 22 and 23 match your NIBA numbers (0.37 to 0.43 s median, flash clusters, zoom-punch on cuts) and 24 and 26 confirm the long slow-motion finish. Add the letterbox option and the one-word-per-cut title.
6. Keep fast cuts for car detailing only. 27 (2 M) is 14 shots in 9 s with flash frames; nothing else in the business niches rewards that pace.
7. Treat whip and zoom-blur transitions as a showreel style, not a client style. The four reels that use them are editors advertising editing, and they sit in the bottom third on likes. Default `transition` to "cut" for everything except sports.
8. Move the CTA out of the frame. 49 of 50 keep the ask in the caption; the only on-screen CTA is a drop date. Your Osh address card still fits (it is a label, not an ask), but "comment SOCIA" belongs in the post text.
9. Drop the end-card habit outside sports. Eight reels have a logo card and all but the sports ones are under 1.5 s; the top-liked business reels end on the payoff frame.
10. Grade is optional. Sixteen reels have a visible grade and none of them are in the top ten of the business niches; the editor's `enhance` step should stay measurement-driven, as it is.
