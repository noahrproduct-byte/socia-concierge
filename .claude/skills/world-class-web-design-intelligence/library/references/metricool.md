## Strongest design qualities
1. **The recommendation is drawn where the decision is made.** "Best times" shades the calendar's own hour slots (darker = better) for one chosen network, with an optional percentage-per-slot toggle [observed in help GIF and screenshot]. **Why it works:** spatial co-location removes the translate-and-remember step between an insight on one screen and an action on another; luminance is read pre-attentively, so the eye finds the dark slot before reading anything. **For SOCIA:** strong fit for the Calendar week view and the composer's time picker, using SOCIA's existing `lib/postingTimes` windows. SOCIA must add what Metricool doesn't visibly do: say where the shading comes from. Metricool's help center admits that without personal data (Instagram not linked through Facebook, Facebook connected after Sep 2024) it shows estimates from general behaviour studies. **In-app (2026-10-19): the UI does not label the source.** The Best times menu offers only "None", one row per connected network, and a "Show as percentage" checkbox; nothing on the calendar or in the menu says where the numbers come from, even for a brand with almost no posting history, whose full 24×7 grid of hourly percentages can only be generic [observed in DOM; source inferred]. Also, in the DOM every one of the 168 hour slots carried the same flat green fill at full opacity, with the percentages kept transparent until the checkbox is on; screenshots weren't possible at that moment, so whether slots are visibly shaded darker for better hours is unconfirmed in this account [measured].
2. **One card is the value, the legend key and the series toggle.** In the real Community chart, the result cards are filled with the series colour; clicking one hides or shows that series, and a hidden one fades [observed in help screenshot + docs]. **Why it works:** it removes the separate legend lookup and makes filtering direct manipulation on the thing you're already reading; the faded state keeps the number visible so nothing looks deleted. **For SOCIA:** Analytics KPI tiles above charts (AnalyticsV3, Facebook, YouTube views). **Confirmed in-app (2026-10-19):** cards are 160×74px, pastel series fill with dark same-hue text, and a hidden series' card drops to opacity 0.35 [measured]. Two gaps to avoid: the cards are plain `div`s with a pointer cursor (no button role, no `aria-pressed`), and their hover tooltip only repeats the label and value [measured].
3. **Output overlaid on outcome.** The Community chart draws the followers line over daily stacked bars of posts, reels and stories [observed in help screenshot]. **Why it works:** juxtaposition invites a causal reading ("I posted a reel, followers moved") without the product claiming causation. **For SOCIA:** Analytics growth chart and the plan loop's "what happened after you posted". Fix their flaws when adapting: the bars have no labelled scale and the follower axis is truncated (136.8K–137.1K), which exaggerates growth [observed].
4. **Comparison follows one stated rule and is absent rather than invented.** Every arrow and % compares against the previous period of equal length; when that history doesn't exist, no percentage is shown; hovering a metric shows the absolute change and the % [observed in help docs]. **Why it works:** a single rule is learnable, and omission avoids fake precision. **For SOCIA:** matches the "—, never 0" rule; adopt the hover that shows absolute delta plus the compared date range. **In-app on Free (2026-10-19):** no KPI card shows any delta or arrow; the "Comparison period" control is premium-marked and opens a "Metricool Studio" upgrade dialog, so comparison is a paid, separate tool [observed]. The single-rule behaviour above could not be verified in-app. The report builder does state its comparison as text under the period ("Compared to" the previous month) [observed].
5. **Plan limits are chunked, scoped and metered in named units.** Cards are cumulative ("everything in X, plus"); the comparison table has category anchor tabs and a sticky CTA row; a limit that differs by platform carries tiny platform icons under its row label (competitors: 5/100 for FB, IG, Twitch, Bluesky; 10 for YouTube; X via add-on); AI is stated as credits "per brand/month", and each AI feature says whether it uses credits or is included [observed]. **Why it works:** chunking keeps a 100-row table navigable, scoping icons answer "on which platform?" at the row, and naming the unit makes cost predictable. **For SOCIA:** /pricing, Settings plan and billing, every `upgradeFor` CTA, and the AI cost model (which features consume metered AI).
6. **Per-network divergence is disclosed only when needed.** In the composer imagery, each selected network icon has its own format chip (e.g. REEL ▾, SHORT ▾) inline in the network row; one shared caption by default; an "edit by network" control splits it; per-network presets sit collapsed below [observed in marketing imagery]. **Why it works:** progressive disclosure: the common case (same post everywhere) stays one field, and format is chosen at the exact point the network is chosen. **For SOCIA:** Create Post composer. SOCIA already splits master content from per-platform tabs; the transferable part is choosing the format inline in the destination row. **Confirmed in the real composer (2026-10-19):** the network glyph is followed by a small uppercase grey format chip (10px/700) with a chevron; its menu lists each format with a one-line description (e.g. a standard video vs short vertical video) [measured]. Per-network presets sit in collapsed rows below the editor ("Global presets", then one per network) [observed]. The shared caption with an "edit by network" split couldn't be checked with only one network connected.
7. **AI refinement is a bounded menu, not a blank prompt.** After generating, the AI panel swaps its input form for one-click transforms (translate, add CTA, add hashtags, lengthen, shorten, change tone, add emojis, correct, optimise for network, change structure), scoped to the last output; the panel header shows remaining credits [observed in marketing imagery]. **Why it works:** recognition over recall, predictable outcomes, and the cost is visible before acting. **For SOCIA:** Generate Caption and AI comment replies; SOCIA keeps its claim checker and verified-context rules on every transform. **In-app (2026-10-19), before generating:** the assistant opens from a toolbar icon in the composer as a second, 990px modal stacked over the composer. Its header reads "Text generator with AI" with "Available AI credits: N of M" on the right and an ⓘ that explains the allotment per brand per month and that it resets on the 1st. The left side is a chat-style area (mascot avatar, a greeting bubble); the right side is a short form: topic (with an example placeholder), tone (emoji + label), language, "Optimize for" network, and "Generate text", disabled until a topic is typed [observed + measured]. The transforms after generation weren't seen because nothing was generated.

## Best UX patterns
- **Exploration vs deliverable split:** help docs state Analytics is for day-to-day exploration and Reporting is for PDFs, PPTs and saved dashboards [observed in help docs]. A clear mental model, although it later fragments (see Weaknesses).
- **Three depths of the same data, named by persona** [observed]: plain-language explanation of each metric with a suggested action ("for those who avoid data"), roles and sharing (teams), CSV and Data Studio (spreadsheet lovers). **Why it works:** it lets one product serve novices and analysts without one compromised screen. **For SOCIA:** every metric gets a plain-language "what this means / what to do" line; exports stay one click away.
- **Usage counter at the point of action** ("Brands 1 of 15") [observed in help screenshot]: the limit is visible where you're about to hit it.
- **Concept explainer where the concept bites** ("What's a brand?" inset on the connections page) [observed].
- **Limits in a three-column help table** (Feature / On Free ✅❌⚠️ / Limit or notes) [observed in help docs]. Clearer than their own pricing page.
- **MCP transparency** [observed]: the MCP page lists every tool with a one-line description and leads with example questions in accordions. Trust comes from inspectability; examples teach capability.

## What makes it feel premium
- **A strict two-colour core:** one dark ink for text, outlines and primary fills, with a single bright accent used only as text on dark CTAs and as highlight bands. Few hues means every accent reads as intentional [measured].
- **Flat surfaces, no shadows**, separated by 1px dark outlines and consistent 16/20px radii. This reads like print, which gives the confidence [measured].
- **Real product UI as hero imagery** instead of abstract illustration, so the claim and the evidence arrive together [observed].
- Where it isn't premium: rainbow wall of saturated brand-coloured CONNECT buttons, slow 0.6s ease-in-out button transitions, default focus outline, stripes and ribbons as decoration [observed/measured].

## Weaknesses
- **Contradictory limit copy:** the analytics page says "No limits" and the planner page says posting is unlimited, while Free is 20 posts/month, 30 days of history and watermarked analytics (help center) [observed]. User-count claims vary between 3M, 3.5M and 4M across pages [observed].
- **Zero conflated with missing:** the analytics hero shows greyed network chips reading "0" for networks that look unconnected [observed in marketing imagery]. The real app hides any section with no data for the period, silently [observed in help docs]. Engagement shown as "0.61" with no unit [observed in marketing imagery]. **Corrected by the logged-in app (2026-10-19):** the real Summary doesn't show unconnected networks as 0; it leaves them out entirely, with no hint that they exist [observed]. An unconnected network's own page is an honest connect empty state, not zeros [observed]. Inside a connected network, though, missing data is handled three different ways: demographic blocks with no data render as about 330px of blank white space under their title with no message, and a competitor the product has no data for shows 0 in every column [observed]. So the problem is real but sits inside connected networks, not in the Summary chips.
- **Numbers that don't add up:** the homepage Summary composite shows a 1.27M total over network chips summing to about 2.13M [observed in marketing imagery].
- **Chart honesty:** truncated follower axis exaggerates small growth; bars with no scale; donut legend truncated ("Promoted (Vie…"); a donut with one 100% slice [observed in help screenshots].
- **Fragmented analytics:** changing the comparison period isn't possible in Analytics; it opens Metricool Studio in a new tab [observed in help docs]. Data lives across Analytics, Studio, Reports, Campaign Dashboards and Data Studio, with an Advanced Analytics add-on on top. **Confirmed in-app (2026-10-19):** the "Comparison period" control on a network page opens an "Unlock Metricool Studio" upgrade dialog, and the Reporting module is a directory of five separate tools (Reports, Campaign dashboards, Studio, Looker Studio, Hashtag Tracker) [observed].
- **Best-time fallback:** generic estimates stand in for personal data on some connections; the help center explains it [observed in help docs]. Confirmed 2026-10-19: the app itself never says so, not in the menu, the calendar or the percentages [observed].
- **Pricing table friction:** the sticky header shows CTA buttons but not plan names or prices; the same long sentence repeats in every cell; three rows all named "Competitor Analysis" differ only by tiny icons; "Unlimited*" depends on a fair-use footnote; annual is the default and cards show only the monthly equivalent [observed].
- **Mobile:** the annual/monthly toggle breaks into a tall pill with "Monthly" dropped below; the comparison becomes one-plan-at-a-time accordions, so side-by-side comparison is lost; the hero product image is clipped [observed at 375px]. No horizontal scroll [measured].
- **Accessibility and semantics:** hero headline isn't the h1; default focus ring; no reduced-motion or dark-mode handling on marketing [measured].
- **Two systems:** marketing (Plus Jakarta Sans + Bodoni) and app (Nunito Sans + Font Awesome on Vuetify) don't share type [measured]. SOCIA has the same split.

## Where SOCIA can do better than Metricool
- **Label every recommendation's source and sample size** (best times, plans). Metricool explains its fallback only in the help center.
- **Never show 0 for unconnected or unknown, and never silently hide a section.** Show "—" plus the reason and the fix.
- **Keep comparison inside Analytics.** No separate tool or tab for "vs previous period" or "vs custom range".
- **State limits once and consistently.** Marketing, pricing, help and in-app upgrade prompts all say the same number. Their help-center table format is the model, not their pricing page.
- **Make AI explain, not just write.** Metricool's assistant is a text generator with transforms; SOCIA's plan loop and verified-context captions are the differentiator. Keep that visible in the UI.
- **Handle missing data the same way everywhere** (added 2026-10-19). Metricool's app mixes blank boxes, zeros for unknown competitors and hidden sections; SOCIA uses "—" with the reason and the fix in every one of those places.
- **One upgrade prompt per view.** Metricool stacks a top-bar button, per-section banners, marked controls and chart watermarks on one page. SOCIA marks gated controls quietly and explains the upgrade only when the person reaches for one.
- **Lead billing with what the person has.** Current plan and usage first, options after; Metricool puts its current-plan card below the upsell.

### Shell and navigation
- **Two-level navigation** [observed + measured]. An 84px aubergine top bar holds the modules (Analytics, Reporting, Inbox, Planning, SmartLinks, Ads) as 48px-tall text tabs (18px/600 white), then the lime "Upgrade your plan" button, the brand avatar (brand switcher), a bell with a red count badge, and a hamburger. Only Analytics has a left sidebar (256px, white): Summary, one row per network, "More connections", and pinned at the bottom Reporting, Reports, Hashtag Tracker and Brand settings plus a collapse button. The other modules use a row of horizontal sub-tabs instead [measured]. **Why it works:** module and sub-section never compete for one column. **For SOCIA:** not the top-bar split (SOCIA's single dark sidebar already carries both levels); the network list as status (next bullet) is the transferable part.
- **Connection state lives in the nav** [observed + measured]. Connected networks show the brand avatar at the row end; unconnected ones show a "+" circle; a network that needs a higher plan gets a lime-tinted row with a lime badge. **Why it works:** the person sees what's connected, what isn't and what's paywalled before opening any page. **For SOCIA:** Analytics platform tabs can carry the same three states (connected, not connected → connect, plan-gated → upgrade) instead of hiding absent platforms.
- **Sticky page header** [measured]: section anchor tabs on the left (uppercase 12px links that jump down the long page) and the labelled "Main period" and "Comparison period" controls on the right, pinned under the top bar (`position: sticky; top: 84px`).
- **Top-bar menus** [observed + measured]. Brand switcher: a 480px popover with "Add brand" first, then each brand as a bold name with its connected network glyphs underneath. ## Patterns NOT relevant to SOCIA
- **Ads campaign management, Hashtag Tracker ($25/day), Data Studio connector, white label, Twitch:** outside SOCIA's scope.
- **Brand-count price ladders inside each plan card:** SOCIA prices by plan tier, not by number of brands.
- **Job-grouped marketing mega menu:** marketing-site navigation; SOCIA's problem is in-app navigation.
- **Flows (comment-to-DM automation) and SmartLinks (link in bio):** not SOCIA features today. The SmartLinks split editor with live phone preview is a generic pattern SOCIA already has (composer PreviewRail).
- **Persona-named marketing sections:** the underlying "three depths" idea is relevant; the persona framing is marketing.
- **Top-bar modules with a per-module sidebar** (logged-in app): SOCIA's single dark sidebar already holds both levels; adding a top bar would split navigation in two.
- **Chart watermarks and per-section upgrade banners as plan enforcement:** they degrade the data people are trying to read; SOCIA limits access, not legibility.
- **Full-page "Loading, please wait" screen:** SOCIA's route skeletons already do better.
