# Legal and platform risk of buying or using third-party-collected Instagram public data (US focus)

Research notes, compiled 2026-11-01. Not legal advice. "Interpretation" marks the researcher's reading, not a holding. "UNVERIFIED" marks claims that come from one weak source or that could not be checked against a primary document. Most court material came through law-firm and press summaries (secondary). Primary text was read directly only for the Meta Platform Terms, the Meta Developer Policies, the Facebook (Meta) Terms of Service and one Meta newsroom post.

## 1. Meta Platforms v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Jan. 23, 2024): the holding, what happened after, and the limits

### Takeaway
Judge Edward Chen granted Bright Data summary judgment on Meta's breach-of-contract claims. He held that the Facebook and Instagram terms, as written then, bound users only while they were using their accounts, so they did not cover logged-off scraping of public data. Meta dismissed its last claim in February 2024 and waived its appeal, so the ruling stands. It is still one unappealed district-court decision on contract wording that Meta has since changed. It does not decide logged-in scraping, anti-hacking claims, DMCA claims or privacy law, and it says nothing about a buyer that holds its own Meta account.

### Cited Findings
- On Jan. 23, 2024, Judge Chen granted Bright Data summary judgment on Meta's breach-of-contract claim, the core of the case, and denied Meta's cross-motion. Meta could show a breach only by proving Bright Data scraped while logged in to a Meta account. — [Farella Braun + Martel (secondary)](https://www.fbm.com/business-litigation/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/392920/judge-sides-against-meta-in-battle-over-scraping-p.html)
- The court read the Facebook and Instagram terms as applying to "users", meaning account holders using their accounts. It cited a 2009 Facebook clause, later removed, that applied "whether or not you are a registered member of Facebook" as evidence that Meta knew how to cover logged-off visitors and had chosen not to. Citation: 2024 WL 251406 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 23, 2024). — [Eric Goldman blog, guest post (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm)
- Even while Bright Data held active accounts, the court found "a strong and compelling argument that Bright Data was not 'using' Facebook" when it scraped logged off. Its logged-off scraping was "unrelated to the purpose of its accounts." — [Eric Goldman blog (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm); [FBM (secondary)](https://www.fbm.com/business-litigation/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)
- Survival clause: the court would not read Meta's survival clause as a lifetime promise. Once Bright Data closed its accounts, Meta could not hold it to a logged-off ban, and courts "should not construe ambiguous writings to create lifetime promises" (quoting M&G Polymers). — [Eric Goldman blog (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm)
- After the ruling: on Feb. 23, 2024, Meta dismissed its remaining claim, tortious interference, without prejudice, and waived its right to appeal the summary-judgment order. Bright Data said it made no agreement with Meta and did not change its conduct. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/26/meta-drops-lawsuit-against-web-scraping-firm-bright-data-that-sold-millions-of-instagram-records); [Eric Goldman blog (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/02/facebook-drops-anti-scraping-lawsuit-against-bright-data-guest-blog-post.htm); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/393996/meta-abandons-lawsuit-against-bright-data-over-scr.html)
- Kieran McCarthy (commentator) called the dismissal close to waving the white flag. — [Eric Goldman blog (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/02/facebook-drops-anti-scraping-lawsuit-against-bright-data-guest-blog-post.htm)
- Before the ruling, Meta had itself paid Bright Data to scrape other websites. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/court-rules-in-favor-of-a-web-scraper-bright-data-which-meta-had-used-and-then-sued); [The Register, 2023](https://www.theregister.com/2023/02/02/meta_web_scraping/)
- What the case does not cover, per commentary: it did not decide whether scraping is lawful in general, and it did not decide CFAA, trespass to chattels, DMCA or misappropriation claims. The commentator declined to treat it as a new baseline for those claims. — [Eric Goldman blog (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm)
- Meta's terms have since changed. The current Facebook (Meta) Terms of Service, effective Jan. 1, 2025, §3.2, say: "You may not access or collect data from our Products using automated means (without our prior permission) or attempt to access data you do not have permission to access, regardless of whether such automated access or collection is undertaken while logged-in to a Facebook account." — [Meta Terms of Service (primary)](https://www.facebook.com/terms.php)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: the "regardless of whether ... logged-in" wording looks written to close the gap Judge Chen found. The ruling depends on the older text and may not carry over to scraping done under the current terms, although the problem of binding someone who never accepted the terms (privity) remains for logged-off scrapers with no account.
- Interpretation: Bright Data was decided on contract grounds alone. A vendor that scraped logged in, used fake accounts, got around technical blocks (a DMCA §1201 theory) or loaded Meta's servers heavily is in a different position. Voyager Labs (fake accounts) and X v. Bright Data (server-impairment claims allowed back in) show those theories survive.
- Interpretation: the ruling protects the scraper on its own facts. It gives a buyer like SOCIA, which holds a Meta account, a business-verified app and accepted Platform Terms, no protection on its own contract with Meta (see section 3).
- Interpretation: one district-court ruling binds no other court, and it does not settle the question in other districts or states.

### Gaps
- Docket number not confirmed from a primary source. I believe it is 3:23-cv-00077-EMC (UNVERIFIED).
- I could not get the full opinion text from CourtListener. The quotes above come through secondary sources.
- I found no source saying exactly when Meta added the "regardless of whether ... logged-in" wording, or whether that was a direct response to Bright Data. The causal link is inference.

## 2. hiQ v. LinkedIn, X Corp. v. Bright Data, and 2025 to 2026 developments

### Takeaway
On the CFAA, the US federal anti-hacking law, the Ninth Circuit reads "without authorization" narrowly for public pages (hiQ, 2022). But hiQ then lost on breach of contract, paid a $500,000 consent judgment, stopped scraping and deleted the data. X v. Bright Data (2024) held that X's contract claims over scraping public posts were preempted by the Copyright Act, then let X back in on claims about access and server impairment. In 2025 Reddit named a customer of scrapers, Perplexity, as a defendant next to the scrapers. That is the clearest sign that downstream buyers can be pulled into these suits.

### Cited Findings
- hiQ, CFAA: on April 18, 2022, after the Supreme Court sent the case back in light of Van Buren, the Ninth Circuit again held that the CFAA likely does not reach scraping of public pages where the "gates" are "up". hiQ Labs, Inc. v. LinkedIn Corp., No. 17-16783. — [Justia, 9th Cir. opinion (primary)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/17-16783/17-16783-2022-04-18.html); [Jenner & Block (secondary)](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/publications/client-alert-data-scraping-in-hiq-v-linkedin-the-ninth-circuit-reaffirms-narrow-interpretation-of-cfaa)
- Sources disagree on the reporter citation: Haynes Boone gives 41 F.4th 1180, Wikipedia gives 31 F.4th 1180. — [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/publications/ninth-circuit-rejects-linkedin-attempt-to-prevent-web-scraping-of-publicly-member-profiles); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn)
- hiQ, contract: in a fall 2022 summary-judgment order, the district court (N.D. Cal., No. 17-cv-03301-EMC, also Judge Chen) held that LinkedIn's user-agreement bans on scraping and fake profiles are enforceable as contract terms. The stipulation dates the order Oct. 27, 2022, while most commentary dates the ruling to the Nov. 4, 2022 hearing. — [Privacy World (secondary)](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2022/12/linkedins-data-scraping-battle-with-hiq-labs-ends-with-proposed-judgment/); [stipulation PDF (primary)](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/23429042/hiq-stip.pdf)
- hiQ, outcome: on Dec. 6, 2022 the parties filed a confidential settlement with a stipulated $500,000 judgment against hiQ and a permanent injunction. hiQ had to stop scraping and delete all source code, data and algorithms obtained from scraping LinkedIn. The court entered the consent judgment on Dec. 8, 2022. — [Proskauer (secondary)](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case); [Morgan Lewis (secondary)](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators)
- X Corp. v. Bright Data Ltd., No. C 23-03698 WHA (N.D. Cal., May 9, 2024): Judge Alsup dismissed X's claims. He held that its contract claims over scraping and selling public posts were preempted by the Copyright Act. X's terms could not give it "de facto copyright ownership" of content its users had made public. — [Skadden (secondary)](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts/district_court_adopts_broad_view_of_copyright_preemption_in_data_scraping_case.pdf); [opinion PDF](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/d/2220/files/2024/09/X-Corp-v-Bright-Data-Ltd.pdf); [Frankfurt Kurnit (secondary)](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102j7s5/blockbuster-ruling-federal-court-holds-that-copyright-act-preempts-xs-web-scrap)
- The same order also dismissed X's access-based claims (trespass to chattels, unfair competition, tortious interference). — [National Law Review (secondary)](https://natlawreview.com/article/x-corp-loses-battle-over-public-data-access)
- On Nov. 26, 2024, Alsup largely let X amend its complaint to add access-related claims, on the theory that Bright Data's access was impairing X's servers. On Dec. 17, 2024, Bright Data answered and filed counterclaims, including antitrust claims. — [Eric Goldman blog, Jan. 2025 (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/01/catching-up-on-the-heavyweight-scraping-battle-between-x-and-bright-data-guest-blog-post.htm)
- Reddit Inc. v. SerpApi LLC, No. 25-cv-08736 (S.D.N.Y.), filed October 2025, sues scrapers SerpApi, Oxylabs and AWMProxy and also Perplexity AI. Perplexity is alleged to be a customer that used the scraped data. Reddit claims violations of the DMCA, among others. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/reddit-sues-perplexity-serpapi-scraping-google-463681); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/reddit-sues-perplexity-others-over-alleged-data-scraping); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/reddit-sues-perplexity-and-three-other-companies-for-allegedly-using-its-content-without-paying-205136436.html)
- UNVERIFIED (single, weak source): on July 31, 2026 a judge largely denied SerpApi's motion to dismiss Reddit's claims, and a separate Google DMCA suit against SerpApi was dismissed on July 20, 2026. — [KuCoin news flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/reddit-lawsuit-against-serpapi-survives-dismissal-motion)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: the US trend for logged-off scraping of public data is that CFAA claims are weak (hiQ), contract claims depend on wording and on who agreed to it (Bright Data, hiQ), and copyright preemption may block contract claims over user content (X, one district judge). Platforms have moved toward DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention, server-impairment and trespass theories, and toward naming customers.
- Interpretation: hiQ is the warning case for a business built on scraped data. It mostly won the public argument on the CFAA but lost on contract, then dissolved, paid $500,000 and deleted its data. The outcome was set by litigation cost and the contract claim, not the CFAA.
- Interpretation: Reddit v. SerpApi, which names Perplexity, is the most relevant precedent for SOCIA's position as a buyer. It shows a platform suing the customer of a scraping vendor alongside the vendor. Reddit is not Meta, and the claims rest on getting around Google's protections, so it is an analogy, not a direct risk signal about Meta.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025 to 2026 ruling or settlement in X Corp. v. Bright Data, and could not confirm whether the May 2024 dismissal was appealed to the Ninth Circuit or how that ended (one source says it was appealed). Status is unknown.
- I could not verify the July 2026 Reddit and Google rulings against SerpApi in a docket or second source, or whether Perplexity's own motion to dismiss has been decided.
- I found no 2025 to 2026 Meta scraping suit against a vendor or customer. That may be a search gap rather than an absence.

## 3. Instagram and Meta terms on automated collection: do they bind a downstream buyer?

### Takeaway
The current Meta (Facebook) Terms ban automated collection "regardless of whether" logged in. They also ban anyone bound by them from purchasing "any data obtained from us or our services", logged in or not. Instagram's Terms of Use have a matching ban on purchasing data. A buyer that never touches Instagram but has accepted Meta's terms, as SOCIA has through its developer account, business portfolio and Instagram accounts, is arguably bound by the purchase ban. That is a separate contract claim from anything against the scraper. Interpretation: this purchase ban, not the automated-collection ban, is SOCIA's main contract exposure.

### Cited Findings
- Meta Terms of Service (effective Jan. 1, 2025), §3.2: "You therefore agree not to engage in the conduct described below (or to facilitate or support others in doing so):" — [Meta Terms (primary)](https://www.facebook.com/terms.php)
- §3.2, automated collection: "You may not access or collect data from our Products using automated means (without our prior permission) or attempt to access data you do not have permission to access, regardless of whether such automated access or collection is undertaken while logged-in to a Facebook account." — [Meta Terms (primary)](https://www.facebook.com/terms.php)
- §3.2, purchasing data: "Except as provided in the Platform Terms, you may not sell, license, or purchase any data obtained from us or our services, regardless of whether such data was obtained while logged-in to a Facebook account." — [Meta Terms (primary)](https://www.facebook.com/terms.php)
- Survival, §4.2: if the account is deleted or the contract ends, the Terms end "except for the following provisions, which will remain in place: (3, 4.2-4.5)." Section 3, including §3.2, is listed as surviving. — [Meta Terms (primary)](https://www.facebook.com/terms.php)
- Instagram Terms of Use (wording from search results and archived copies; I could not load the live page, which needs JavaScript): users may not access or collect information in unauthorized ways, "including ... collecting information in an automated way without our express permission". They may not "sell, license, or purchase any account or data obtained from us or our Service." — [Instagram Help Center, Terms of Use](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870); [Open Terms Archive memo](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/instagram-blocks-data-retrieval-and-clarifies-what-information-it-retrieves/). Exact current wording: UNVERIFIED.
- In Bright Data, the court held that the old terms bound account holders only while they were using their accounts, and that Meta could not extend them by a survival clause to unrelated logged-off conduct after the accounts closed. — [Eric Goldman blog (secondary)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm)
- In the Voyager Labs case, the court found it "plausible to infer that Voyager's continued use of the social media platforms signified its assent to those terms". — quoted in [MediaPost search summary (secondary)](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401661/analytics-company-voyager-labs-settles-scraping-ba.html)
- In X v. Bright Data, contract claims over scraping and reselling public user content were held preempted by the Copyright Act. — [Skadden (secondary)](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts/district_court_adopts_broad_view_of_copyright_preemption_in_data_scraping_case.pdf)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: a buyer with no Meta relationship at all would likely be outside Meta's contract, the same privity problem that sank Meta against logged-off Bright Data. SOCIA is not that buyer. It operates a Meta developer app, a verified business and connected Instagram and Facebook accounts, so it has accepted the Meta Terms and the Platform Terms. The purchase ban is drafted to apply "regardless of whether such data was obtained while logged-in", which goes beyond what the court read the old terms to cover.
- Interpretation: SOCIA's arguments would include that the purchase ban applies only to SOCIA's "use" of Meta Products under its accounts, the Bright Data "use" reasoning. Buying a dataset from a vendor arguably has nothing to do with SOCIA's accounts. But SOCIA would use the data inside the same product that runs its Meta app, which makes the "unrelated to the accounts" argument weaker than Bright Data's was. A further argument is copyright preemption on the X v. Bright Data theory, though that rests on one judge in a different case. Both arguments are untested for a buyer.
- Interpretation: "facilitate or support others" is a second route. Paying a vendor to keep scraping Instagram could be framed as supporting the vendor's violation, although after Bright Data a logged-off vendor may not be violating anything. This also mostly matters as grounds for platform enforcement (section 4), not damages.
- Interpretation: even where Meta's contract claim against SOCIA is weak in court, Meta does not need to win a lawsuit to restrict SOCIA's app (section 4). The business risk is larger than the litigation risk.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the live Instagram Terms of Use text (the page renders client-side). The Instagram wording and its effective date need checking in a browser.
- I found no case applying a platform's "purchase" ban to a downstream buyer. Whether that clause can be enforced against a buyer is untested as far as these sources show.

## 4. Meta Platform Terms and Developer Policies: could third-party Instagram data inside SOCIA lead to enforcement against the Meta app?

### Takeaway
The Platform Terms do not mention scraped data or combining Platform Data with outside data in so many words. They do require compliance with Meta's other terms, which include the purchase ban in section 3. They also let Meta act against an app "in our sole discretion" when it believes the developer "may have violated" its terms or that action protects Meta from legal or regulatory liability. Enforcement can mean suspending or removing the app and account and ordering deletion of Platform Data. Interpretation: the main risk to SOCIA's app is discretionary enforcement or a stalled App Review, not a court ruling.

### Cited Findings (Meta Platform Terms, last updated Feb. 3, 2026, primary)
- §1.b: "To use Platform (including to Process any Platform Data), you agree to these Platform Terms". The section also requires compliance with "all other applicable terms and policies" (paraphrased). §1.c requires compliance with the Developer Policies, the Developer Docs and "all applicable laws and regulations". — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- §11.c: where terms conflict, the one "more restrictive on you and your App or more protective of us" applies (paraphrase plus fragment). — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- §12.l: "'Platform Data' means any information, data, or other content you obtain from us, through Platform or through your App". It extends to data obtained directly or indirectly, and to anonymized, aggregated or derived data (paraphrased). — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- §3.a: "You will not perform, or facilitate or support others in performing, any of the following prohibited practices". These include §3.a.iii "Processing Platform Data to perform, facilitate, or provide tools for surveillance." They also include §3.a.iv "Selling, licensing, or purchasing Platform Data." and §3.a.v "Processing Platform Data without valid User consent in order to build or augment user profiles for any purpose." — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- §3.a.vii restricts materially changing the scope of processing of Platform Data without resubmitting for App Review (paraphrase). §3.a.viii: "Processing Platform Data for purposes other than the applicable permitted purposes set forth in Meta's Developer Docs." — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- §7.e.i: "We may take enforcement action against you and your App if we believe, in our sole discretion, that:" (grounds paraphrased): (1) you have not responded on time to monitoring or audit requests; (2) you or your App has violated, or may have violated, the Terms or other policies, or is harming the Platform, other Meta Products, Platform Data or users; (3) you built or kept apps to get around enforcement; (4) action is needed to comply with law or a court or government order; (5) action is needed to protect Meta from legal or regulatory liability. — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- §7.e.ii: enforcement "can include suspending or permanently removing your App(s) and account". It can also require you to "stop Processing and delete Platform Data." — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- §7.c.i: "We or Third-Party Auditors may conduct an Audit, no more than once a calendar year unless there is a Necessary Condition". §7.c.ii requires at least 10 business days' notice unless a Necessary Condition applies. §7.c.vi makes you reimburse reasonable audit costs if the audit finds non-compliance. — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)
- The Platform Terms contain no clause that expressly addresses scraping, data brokers or combining Platform Data with data from other sources (checked by reading the text). — [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)

### Cited Findings (Meta Developer Policies, last updated Feb. 3, 2026, primary)
- §5.3(c), Tech Providers: "Don't facilitate or encourage any violations of our policies." — [Meta Developer Policies](https://developers.facebook.com/devpolicy/)
- §10.7: "Don't use Meta's data to build or augment any user profiles." — [Meta Developer Policies](https://developers.facebook.com/devpolicy/)
- §3.5: "we may take enforcement action against you or your App(s) for failing to do so." — [Meta Developer Policies](https://developers.facebook.com/devpolicy/)
- The Developer Policies contain no explicit clause on scraping, on data from third-party sources, or on Data Use Checkup. — [Meta Developer Policies](https://developers.facebook.com/devpolicy/)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: data a vendor scraped from Instagram probably is not "Platform Data", which covers data obtained "from us, through Platform or through your App". So §3.a.iv (no purchasing Platform Data) probably does not apply directly. The phrase "from us" is broad enough that Meta could argue otherwise. The derived-data language is also a risk if SOCIA mixes vendor data with Graph API data in one record. For example, enriching a competitor profile pulled through Business Discovery with scraped Reel view counts could be framed as "augmenting" a profile built from Platform Data.
- Interpretation: the bridge from the consumer terms to app enforcement is §1.b ("all other applicable terms and policies") plus §7.e.i ground 2 ("may have violated" ... "other policies") and ground 5 (protecting Meta from liability). The Meta Terms' ban on purchasing "any data obtained from us or our services" is one of those other applicable terms. Meta needs only to "believe, in our sole discretion" that a violation may have occurred. There is no court test.
- Interpretation, App Review: App Review submissions describe how each permission's data is used. If SOCIA's competitor features visibly show Instagram data that Meta's APIs do not provide, such as Reel view counts for accounts SOCIA does not manage, reviewers or later audits may ask where it came from. Showing it next to Meta-sourced data without clear labelling also clashes with SOCIA's own honesty standard. That last point is from project context, not a Meta source.
- Interpretation: the realistic worst case is suspension or removal of the app and the developer account, plus an order to delete Platform Data. For SOCIA that would cut off Instagram publishing and analytics for all customers, a risk to the whole business that is out of proportion to the value of the competitor feature.
- Interpretation: lower-risk alternatives are Meta's own sanctioned routes. Business Discovery through the Instagram Graph API returns some public business and creator account fields, but per project notes not Reel view counts for other accounts. Another option is data that competitors or creators share with SOCIA themselves through OAuth. The project's own data-reality notes cover this; it is not sourced here.

### Gaps
- I found no public instance of Meta restricting or rejecting a developer app because it used scraped or third-party Instagram data. Meta does not publish App Review denial reasons or enforcement case lists for developers. The likelihood is unknown.
- Data Use Checkup (Meta's annual self-certification) is not described in either document. Its current questions could not be checked.
- I did not read Meta's Developer Docs "permitted purposes" for each Instagram permission, which §3.a.viii relies on.

## 5. Meta's enforcement history against scraping vendors, and whether customers were pursued

### Takeaway
Meta has a long record of suing scraping vendors and scrapers-for-hire: Massroot8 (2020), Social Data Trading, BrandTotal/Unimania, Octopus and Ekrem Ateş (2022), Voyager Labs (2023) and Bright Data (2023). Most ended in permanent injunctions, deletion orders and undisclosed payments, Bright Data being the exception. No source found shows Meta suing a vendor's customer. Meta has said it wants to deter "those who buy" these services, and in 2025 Reddit sued a customer of scrapers.

### Cited Findings
- Massroot8: Meta settled with the scraping service Massroot8 in 2020. Terms not found. — [TechCrunch 2022 summary](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/03/meta-settles-lawsuit-for-significant-sum-against-businesses-scraping-facebook-and-instagram-data)
- Social Data Trading (Hong Kong): Meta, through Instagram, alleged the company offered customers data scraped from Instagram profiles. Meta sent a cease-and-desist, and a principal replied that the company had a right to access the information. Sources variously date the matter to 2021 or 2022; outcome not found. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/369624/instagram-sues-analytics-company-social-data-tradi.html)
- BrandTotal/Unimania: on summary judgment the court held BrandTotal breached the Facebook terms of use but did not violate the CFAA. In October 2022 the companies settled, agreeing to a permanent injunction against scraping Facebook and Instagram or profiting from collected data, and to pay a sum Meta called "significant" but did not disclose. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/03/meta-settles-lawsuit-for-significant-sum-against-businesses-scraping-facebook-and-instagram-data)
- Octopus Data and Ekrem Ateş (July 2022): Meta said Octopus offers "scraping services and access to software that customers can use to scrape any website" (Octoparse) and is part of "an emerging scraping industry that provides automation services to any customer." Meta alleged violations of its Terms and the DMCA and sought a permanent injunction. Ateş allegedly used automated Instagram accounts to scrape more than 350,000 profiles for clone sites. Meta disabled his accounts, sent a cease-and-desist and revoked his access. Meta's post says it aims to "deter the abuse of these capabilities both among those who sell them and those who buy them." — [Meta newsroom, "Taking Action Against Scraping for Hire" (primary)](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/07/actions-against-scraping-for-hire/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/meta-sues-data-scraping-company-octopus-213001115.html)
- Voyager Labs (filed Jan. 2023, N.D. Cal., Judge Martínez-Olguín): Meta alleged Voyager created about 38,000 fake Facebook accounts and scraped data from more than 600,000 users. The claims were federal and California anti-hacking laws plus breach of contract. The court denied Voyager's personal-jurisdiction motion in 2024. Voyager later settled, agreeing to stop scraping Facebook and Instagram and to permanently delete data collected from Meta. Settlement date and money terms not found. — [Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/meta-can-proceed-with-data-scraping-suit-against-uk-surveillance-startup); [MediaPost (settlement)](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401661/analytics-company-voyager-labs-settles-scraping-ba.html); [Law360 headline](https://law360.com/articles/1536075)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: Meta's litigation pattern targets the people doing the scraping, especially those using fake or logged-in accounts, those selling surveillance tools and clone sites. It does not target legitimate businesses that buy marketing analytics. Being sued is therefore a lower risk for SOCIA than platform enforcement. The pattern is not a promise.
- Interpretation: Meta has two ways to act against a buyer that the earlier defendants lacked against Meta: the contract SOCIA has accepted, and the app SOCIA depends on. Customers of BrandTotal or Octopus generally had no such link to Meta. That makes SOCIA an easier target than a typical customer, although no precedent shows Meta using that leverage.
- Interpretation: the settled cases ended with deletion orders covering scraped data. If a vendor SOCIA relies on settles with Meta in the same way, its data feed could stop with no warning, and data already delivered could become contested. That is supply risk as well as legal risk.

## 6. Privacy law (GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, Illinois BIPA) and copyright

### Takeaway
"Publicly available" helps in the US but is not a blanket exemption. GDPR regulators treat scraped public personal data as still regulated, and the Dutch authority has said Dutch organizations may not use Clearview's scraped-data service, a direct model for buyer liability. BIPA applies if SOCIA analyzes faces in Reels or thumbnails, whatever the source. Stored Reels and thumbnails remain the copyright of their creators. Thumbnail-style uses have a fair-use line of cases, while storing or showing full videos has much less support.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR, Clearview: on May 16, 2024 the Dutch Data Protection Authority (AP) fined Clearview AI €30.5 million, with up to €5.1 million more for non-compliance. It found Clearview's business interests were not a "legitimate interest" under Art. 6(1)(f). Commentators note regulators were clear that data being "publicly available" is not a legal basis. — [Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2024-10-16/netherlands-face-recognition-company-clearview-ai-fined-for-violating-eus-general-data-protection-regulation/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/03/clearview-ai-hit-with-its-largest-gdpr-fine-yet-as-dutch-regulator-considers-holding-execs-personally-liable); [Privacy Bootcamp (secondary)](https://www.privacybootcamp.com/Resources/Article/clearview-ai-fine-when-publicly-available-becomes-unlawfully-processed)
- The AP also warned that Dutch organizations may not use Clearview's services, a regulator treating customers of a scraped-data provider as potentially liable. Several other EU authorities (Austria, France, Germany, Italy, Greece) had already found Clearview in violation. — [search summary of the AP announcement](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/current/dutch-dpa-imposes-a-fine-on-clearview-because-of-illegal-data-collection-for-facial-recognition); [Privacy Company (secondary)](https://www.privacycompany.eu/blog/extraterritorial-enforcement-of-the-gdpr-in-light-of-clearview-ais-recent-fine-)
- CCPA/CPRA: the CPRA widened "publicly available" from government records alone to include information a business reasonably believes the consumer lawfully made available to the general public, or that comes from widely distributed media. A commentator notes that private accounts and posts may fall outside it. — [ByteBack Law (secondary)](https://www.bytebacklaw.com/2022/01/how-do-the-cpra-cpa-vcdpa-treat-publicly-available-information/); [TrueVault (secondary)](https://www.truevault.com/learn/ccpa/what-is-publicly-available-information-under-the-ccpa)
- A California Senate Judiciary analysis (2024) describes a bill that would remove information gathered by automated extraction techniques from the "publicly available" exception. Bill number and status: UNVERIFIED (the PDF could not be read). — [CA Senate Judiciary analysis](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/media/1543)
- BIPA (Illinois, since 2008): a company may not collect a biometric identifier, including face geometry, without first informing the person and getting consent. — [LexBlog (secondary)](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=2239364)
- Canadian courts (Alberta and British Columbia, 2025) read "publicly available" narrowly to exclude scraped social media in the Clearview matters. These are persuasive only. — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2025/04/bc-court-requires-clearview-ais-facial-recognition-software-to-comply-with-provincial-privacy-laws); [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2025/alberta-court-clarifies-consent-and-publicly-available-info)
- Copyright, thumbnails: in Kelly v. Arriba Soft (9th Cir.), reduced thumbnails of copied images in a search engine were fair use as transformative. Displaying full-size images was not covered. — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_v._Arriba_Soft_Corp.); [WilmerHale (secondary)](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/publications/thumbnails-fair-use-and-hyperlinks-the-ninth-circuit-breaks-new-ground-in-kelly-v-arriba-soft-february-20-2002)
- Copyright, ownership: X v. Bright Data noted that the platform does not own its users' content. Copyright in posts and Reels stays with their creators, and the creators can enforce it. — [Skadden (secondary)](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts/district_court_adopts_broad_view_of_copyright_preemption_in_data_scraping_case.pdf)
- DMCA: Meta's Octopus suit and Reddit's SerpApi suit both use DMCA theories against scrapers. — [Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/07/actions-against-scraping-for-hire/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/reddit-sues-perplexity-serpapi-scraping-google-463681)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: aggregate public business-account metrics (follower counts, Reel view counts, posting cadence) carry the lowest privacy risk. Profiles of individual creators are personal data under GDPR, and plausibly under CCPA once the "publicly available" exception is tested. Including EU accounts would need a documented legitimate-interest assessment and transparency. Clearview shows a regulator going after the customer side.
- Interpretation: CCPA applies to SOCIA only above its statutory thresholds; the thresholds were not researched here. Data-broker registration rules (California Delete Act) may matter if SOCIA resells or redistributes the data. Neither was checked; both are UNVERIFIED as applied to SOCIA.
- Interpretation: face analysis of Reels or thumbnails (for example "faces in the first second" signals) could trigger BIPA for Illinois residents. BIPA allows private suits with statutory damages, so it is the sharpest US privacy risk. The simplest fix is to keep face geometry out of any feature built on third-party video.
- Interpretation: storing or redisplaying full competitor Reels from a vendor is the highest copyright risk. Storing metrics, links and the platform's embed (rather than copies) is the lowest. Thumbnails in an analytics interface have Kelly-style arguments but are not risk-free.
