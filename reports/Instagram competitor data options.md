# Let customers' own Meta logins power Discover

**The compliant engine for SOCIA's Discover feature is each customer's own Instagram connection, moved to Meta's new Instagram API with Unified Login, which needs no Facebook Page, plus pasted links rendered as official embeds for everyone else.** Unified Login is Meta's recommended setup, accepts Instagram credentials alone, and Meta's guide pages say it supports both Business Discovery (other professional accounts' follower counts plus per-post likes, comments and views) and Hashtag Search. But Business Discovery's own reference page still says it is Facebook Login only, so the one call SOCIA needs most is **unverified until SOCIA makes it live**, which it can do this week in Development mode at no cost. Every other route is worse. Third-party data vendors are scraping by another name, and buying from them arguably breaches the Meta Terms SOCIA has already accepted, putting at risk the single Meta app that carries every customer's Instagram connection and publishing. A central SOCIA research account conflicts with several Platform Terms clauses and gets only 30 hashtags a week for the whole customer base. Meta Content Library is closed to for-profit companies. On cost the Meta-official routes charge no API fees at any scale, while vendors run roughly $10 to $2,200 a month at 100 customers and $50 to $5,400 at 1,000. One hard limit applies to every compliant route: no official API searches for "accounts like mine", so new competitor accounts must come from usernames that customers or SOCIA's web-based suggestions supply, then get checked through Business Discovery. This report is research, not legal advice. Items marked UNVERIFIED rest on contradictory or missing primary sources.

## Unified Login could give every customer competitor data without a Page

The design SOCIA has been waiting for appeared in Meta's docs around September to October 2026. Meta now lists three Instagram API setups and says plainly that "Instagram API with Unified Login is recommended for new integrations," and that app users connect "by logging in with either Instagram or Facebook, without a Facebook Page" ([Instagram Platform overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/overview)). New features ship only there, and **newly created apps can no longer set up Instagram Login at all** ([Unified Login](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login); [Migration guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/migration-guide)). That is the direction of travel for SOCIA's whole Instagram integration, not just Discover. Meta has set no retirement date for Instagram Login ([Migrate to Unified Login](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/migration)).

Under the hood, Unified Login runs on Facebook Login for Business. The customer signs in, picks a business portfolio and an Instagram account, and SOCIA receives a **Business Integration System User token that belongs to the customer's portfolio and can be set never to expire**. Calls go to `graph.facebook.com` ([Authentication](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/authentication)). The new friction is the portfolio picker, not a Page. Meta says "Every Instagram professional account already has one," yet warns that many clients "think of their business as just their Instagram account" ([Business onboarding](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/business-onboarding)). What an Instagram-only café owner actually sees at that step is UNVERIFIED, and it is the likeliest place a real customer gets stuck.

Business Discovery is the capability that matters. Its guide says it is "available with both" Facebook Login and Unified Login, and returns another professional account's `followers_count`, `media_count` and per-media `like_count`, `comments_count` and `view_count`, where "view_count includes both paid and organic metrics" ([Business Discovery guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-discovery)). The contradiction: the endpoint reference still says "Available for the Instagram API with Facebook Login" and asks for a Facebook User token with `pages_read_engagement`, a Page permission ([business_discovery reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/business_discovery)). Meta's feature matrix for Unified Login lists hashtag search, insights, publishing and comments, and **has no Business Discovery row** ([overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/overview)). Across Meta's pages, the guides were updated for Unified Login while the reference pages lag, so Business Discovery probably works. "Probably" is not good enough for SOCIA's most important call. No developer has publicly reported a successful call. One adopter that merged Unified Login on November 1, 2026 had "tested against fakes only" ([staqly PR #197](https://github.com/staqly/staqly/pull/197)).

Hashtag Search is better documented (the matrix marks it "Supported") but harder to get. It needs the **Instagram Public Content Access** feature, which "requires successful completion of the App Review process before your app can access live data" and business verification ([Feature reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/features-reference/instagram-public-content-access)). Its allowed uses centre on a brand's own campaigns and sentiment, but the list also includes helping users "develop their content strategy", which a per-customer niche trend feed fits. Each Instagram account gets **30 unique hashtags per rolling 7 days**, and `recent_media` covers only the last 24 hours, returns no `username`, and lists no view count among its fields ([recent_media](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-hashtag/recent-media); [Hashtag Search guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/hashtag-search)). So hashtag results give SOCIA trending Reels with likes and comments, not a list of who posted them. The **Audio API** is a bonus: with no search term it returns "trending original sounds or music", and it says it works under Unified Login, although its own "Before you start" box still asks for a Page ([Audio API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/content-publishing/audio-api)).

The costs are App Review and migration work, not money. SOCIA's current Advanced Access covers the `instagram_business_*` permissions, which map to a different family (`instagram_basic`, `instagram_manage_insights`, `instagram_content_publish`) that needs its own review unless SOCIA's Facebook side already holds it ([Permissions](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/permissions)). Meta's default configuration marks `instagram_manage_messages` as required, and it is unclear whether SOCIA, which sends no DMs, can drop it ([Get started](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/get-started)). Each customer has to reconnect once, since "Instagram User access tokens don't work with" Unified Login, and the app must be owned by a business portfolio and use Facebook Login for Business rather than classic Facebook Login ([Migration guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/migration-guide)). Some Facebook-Login-only features do not carry over, including `collaborative_media`, which SOCIA's collaborator feature reads ([IG User reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user)). The low-risk rollout is the one Meta's guide allows: keep Instagram Login for publishing and analytics, and add Unified Login as an optional "unlock competitor research" connection. Meta frames running both as temporary, so whether a long-term dual connection is fine is UNVERIFIED.

On platform risk, this route fits Meta's rules best because every call runs on the customer's own token, on that customer's behalf, inside the permissions' stated purpose. One rule shapes the build: if Meta treats SOCIA as a Tech Provider, each Client's Platform Data must be "maintained separately from that of other Clients" ([Platform Terms §5.b.ii](https://developers.facebook.com/terms)). So SOCIA should not cache one customer's Business Discovery results and serve them to another customer who tracks the same account. Because the calls are free, skipping that saving costs nothing. Rate limits stop mattering at this load. Business Discovery falls under Platform Rate Limits (200 calls an hour times daily active users, app-wide, plus an unpublished per-token budget) ([Rate limiting](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/overview/rate-limiting)), and five competitors a week is five calls per customer token.

## A central SOCIA research account collides with five Meta clauses

The tempting shortcut, one SOCIA-owned Instagram account linked to a Page whose token feeds a shared research database, is technically easy and contractually exposed. Results collected with SOCIA's token and shown to paying customers count as Platform Data, a term that covers data "anonymized, aggregated, or derived" from it. Showing that data to customers is sharing it with third parties. Under the Terms, sharing is allowed only in a closed list of cases, and the only one that fits requires the data not to be Restricted Platform Data and every customer to be bound by contract ([Platform Terms §3.c, §12](https://developers.facebook.com/terms)). A paid pool could also be read as "Selling, licensing, or purchasing Platform Data" (§3.a.iv). Weekly snapshots of the same accounts could be read as building "user profiles" without consent (§3.a.v). And a pool shared across customers directly contradicts the Tech Provider separation duty (§5.b.ii.2). No Meta clause names this design, and no public enforcement action targets it, but Meta enforces API terms mostly through private restrictions and audits, so silence proves nothing.

Access levels close the remaining gap. Standard Access, which needs no App Review, is for an app that "only serves your Instagram professional account or an account you manage" ([Instagram Platform overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/overview)). Serving the results to customers without app roles is the case Meta says needs Advanced Access. Hashtag Search needs App Review regardless. Then there is scale: **30 unique hashtags a week, likely per account, for SOCIA's entire customer base** ([ig-hashtag-search](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-hashtag-search)) cannot cover hundreds of niches. Adding research accounts to multiply that limit risks the "respect ... the limits" and "authentic account" policies and the circumvention ground for enforcement ([Developer Policies](https://developers.facebook.com/devpolicy); [Platform Terms §7.e.i.3](https://developers.facebook.com/terms)). Business Discovery volume would fit (about 21,700 calls a month at 1,000 customers is about 30 an hour), but only if the unpublished per-token budget allows it; forum figures of 200 an hour are UNVERIFIED. The API fees are zero. The real cost is that a strike against this feature lands on the same app ID that carries every customer's publishing.

## Data vendors are scraping by another name, and SOCIA is the bound party

Vendors split into influencer-data platforms and raw data APIs. Neither group gets its Instagram data from Meta in a way that covers per-Reel numbers for accounts that never authorized them, because Meta's APIs do not offer that to third parties. Apify states outright that its official actor "sees the logged-out version of the page" ([Apify Instagram Scraper](https://apify.com/apify/instagram-scraper)). Data365 offers "any public info that doesn't require login" ([Data365](https://data365.co/instagram)). EnsembleData says "Every request triggers a live scrape" ([EnsembleData](https://ensembledata.com/instagram-api)). Influencers.club fetches "in real-time when you request them" ([Influencers.club pricing](https://influencers.club/pricing/)). Modash's Raw API serves posts and Reels "the moment it goes public" without naming a source ([Modash API](https://www.modash.io/influencer-marketing-api)). HikerAPI calls itself an "Unofficial Mobile API" ([HikerAPI](https://hikerapi.com/)), and PhantomBuster runs on the customer's own session cookie ([PhantomBuster](https://phantombuster.com/automations/instagram/7085/instagram-profile-scraper)). Both of those break SOCIA's rules outright. Creator-authorized platforms such as Phyllo get data only from creators who connect, so they cannot cover competitors ([Phyllo](https://www.getphyllo.com/resource/instagram-apis-for-developers)).

Capability is real but uneven. Apify documents `videoPlayCount`, `videoViewCount`, likes, comments, caption and timestamp per Reel, plus hashtag and keyword modes ([Apify Reel Scraper](https://apify.com/apify/instagram-reel-scraper); [Hashtag Scraper](https://apify.com/apify/instagram-hashtag-scraper)). ScrapeCreators warns that "Play counts may be inaccurate" ([ScrapeCreators](https://scrapecreators.com/instagram-api)). No primary schema page confirmed per-Reel play counts for Modash or Influencers.club. **No vendor offers related-account discovery from a confirmed source**, since Apify removed `relatedProfiles` on September 14, 2026. Breakage is constant: in October 2026 many Apify runs returned only a profile's first pages, and Instagram stopped sending `reshareCount` ([Apify changelog](https://apify.com/apify/instagram-scraper/changelog)). Every vendor's terms put legal compliance on the customer ([Apify terms](https://docs.apify.com/legal/general-terms-and-conditions); [ScrapeCreators terms](https://scrapecreators.com/terms); [EnsembleData terms](https://ensembledata.com/terms-and-conditions)).

The legal picture is where SOCIA differs from an ordinary buyer. Meta lost *Meta v. Bright Data* in January 2024 because its terms then bound account holders only while using their accounts, so logged-off scraping fell outside them. Meta dropped the case ([Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/26/meta-drops-lawsuit-against-web-scraping-firm-bright-data-that-sold-millions-of-instagram-records)). That win protects the scraper. **Meta's current Terms (effective January 1, 2025) ban anyone bound by them from purchasing "any data obtained from us or our services, regardless of whether such data was obtained while logged-in," and that section survives account deletion** ([Meta Terms §3.2, §4.2](https://www.facebook.com/terms.php)). SOCIA is bound through its developer account, verified business and connected accounts. No court has applied a purchase ban to a downstream buyer, but Meta does not need a court. It can suspend or remove an app and order deletion of Platform Data whenever it believes "in our sole discretion" that the developer "may have violated" its terms ([Platform Terms §7.e](https://developers.facebook.com/terms/)). Meta has said it wants to deter scraping services "both among those who sell them and those who buy them" ([Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/07/actions-against-scraping-for-hire/)). Reddit's October 2025 suit named Perplexity, a customer of scrapers, next to the scrapers themselves ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/reddit-sues-perplexity-serpapi-scraping-google-463681)). hiQ largely won its CFAA argument, lost on contract, paid $500,000 and deleted everything ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case)). Privacy law adds risk at the edges. The Dutch regulator fined Clearview €30.5 million and told Dutch organizations they may not use its service ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2024-10-16/netherlands-face-recognition-company-clearview-ai-fined-for-violating-eus-general-data-protection-regulation/)), and Illinois BIPA reaches any face analysis on third-party Reels ([LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=2239364)).

The best vendor contract read was Modash's, which allows "derivative products" for API customers but bans redistributing the data and requires deleting all of it on termination, so SOCIA's history charts would vanish if it cancelled ([Modash ToS](https://www.modash.io/legal/terms-of-service)). That is a manageable commercial term. The purchase ban is not. **In substance, buying from these vendors is outsourced scraping, and it puts the core product's Meta app on the line for a secondary feature.**

## Links and Content Library fill gaps but cannot run an engine

User-submitted links are the universal, zero-risk floor, and they are thinner than they look. Since June 15, 2026, Meta's oEmbed APIs work **without a token or App Review, at up to 1,000 requests per endpoint per hour**. Apps approved for Meta oEmbed Read get 5 million a day ([oEmbed Post reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/reference/oembed-post/)). The response is the embed `html` and little else, because Meta removed author and thumbnail fields on November 3, 2025 ([instagram-oembed reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/reference/instagram-oembed/); [changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/non-versioned-changes/nvc-2025)). Meta also prohibits "consuming, manipulating, extracting, or persisting the metadata and content" and "deriving information ... for analytics purposes" ([Embed an Instagram Post](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/oembed/)). So SOCIA can store the URL the user typed, render the live embed at view time, and analyze only what the user adds: their notes, a caption they paste, or their answers to a short hook, format and call-to-action checklist, labelled as theirs. No official endpoint looks up another account's post by URL ([Business Discovery guide](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-discovery)). With a Unified Login token, though, SOCIA can take the username from a pasted profile link, call Business Discovery, and show real numbers. Without one, the honest state is "Instagram doesn't share numbers for other accounts' posts", never a zero. Uploaded competitor videos are a further step that needs legal review: transient analysis that keeps only SOCIA's own structural notes is the defensible version ([U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)). Fetching Reel files from Instagram's embed page, a trick used in rater training, likely conflicts with the extraction ban and should not become a product feature.

Meta Content Library is a dead end for SOCIA. Applicants must belong to "A not-for-profit institution (in other words, not a business whose sole purpose is to make a profit)," and API analysis happens only in cleanrooms ([Content Library: Get access](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/content-library-and-api/get-access); [Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/researchtools/meta-content-library)). Even a university partner could not pipe that data into a commercial product. Meta offers no other trend API. Reels Trends lives only in the Instagram mobile app's Professional Dashboard ([IZEA](https://izea.com/?p=317064)), so SOCIA can point users there and let them paste what they find. The Creator Marketplace API is limited to eligible brands seeking partnership-ads creators ([Meta developer blog](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/09/09/instagrams-creator-marketplace-and-facebook-creator-discovery-apis-are-launching-soon-get-ready/)).

## Conclusion

The recommendation is to build Discover on option 3, with option 4 underneath it for everyone, and to build neither option 2 nor option 1. The engine has three parts. The customer, or SOCIA's existing prompt and web suggestions, proposes candidate usernames. Business Discovery on the customer's own Unified Login token verifies them and returns real counts. Hashtag Search and the Audio API on that same token supply trending Reels and sounds for the customer's niche, within 30 hashtags a week. Customers who don't connect get pasted links, live embeds and their own notes, and the page says plainly what Meta does not share. This is also the migration Meta is pushing every Instagram integration toward, so the Discover work doubles as groundwork for leaving Instagram Login. The trade is honesty about gaps: no "accounts like yours" search, Reel views only for professional accounts that aren't age-gated, and likes and comments but no views on hashtag Reels (UNVERIFIED for `top_media`). That fits SOCIA's own standard that unavailable data is never shown as zero. It may also clear the Development-mode blocker that parked SOCIA's Facebook connection, because Unified Login testers accept invites on Instagram's website rather than in an app (UNVERIFIED until step 3 below works).

The next step costs nothing and settles the biggest unknown. Run the test in this order, and do not file App Review until the calls themselves answer.
